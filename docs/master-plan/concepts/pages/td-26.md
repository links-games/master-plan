**The idea:** Three big woven baskets, coral, yellow and sky blue, sit in the clover; the child drags a round blue pebble into the blue basket and it plops in with a cheerful sparkle.

| Decision             | Proposal                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiar mechanic    | Color sorting                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Learning objective   | Color names, sorting by color                                                                                                                                                               |
| First prototype      | One authored challenge built around the interaction above, with a clear goal, immediate feedback, restart and unlimited retries.                                                            |
| Technology           | React + SVG/DOM; deterministic state, undo and seeded levels. Start with one local screen and touch plus keyboard input.                                                                    |
| Scope boundary       | One board and one rule set; defer accounts, multiplayer, procedural content and platform SDKs.                                                                                              |

| Main uncertainty     | Test whether the lesson follows from play and whether the first interaction is understandable without reading.                                                                              |

## Story

The wind blew and blew. Leaves and petals and pebbles tumbled across the meadow. What a jumble! Linky found three baskets: red, yellow and blue. A red petal went into the red basket. A yellow leaf went into the yellow basket. A blue pebble went into the blue basket. Soon everything was in its place. The meadow looked neat and bright, and the wind said sorry with a soft whoosh.

> **Wisdom:** Everything has its place.

### Gameplay

Three big woven baskets, coral, yellow and sky blue, sit in the clover; the child drags a round blue pebble into the blue basket and it plops in with a cheerful sparkle.

### World

A corner of the wild garden by the meadow, with berry bushes, flowers and the short white Chord tower in the soft background. Late summer light, warm and golden. Everything grows wild, and the things to sort are fallen treasures blown by the wind, never picked from living plants.

### Characters and materials

Things to sort: fallen leaves, petals, pebbles, feathers, toy balls and copied berries in coral, sunshine yellow, leaf green and sky blue. The baskets are white lattice with one big color band each. Soru and Tari cheer from the basket rims. Each color also has its own shape, so color is never the only clue.

### Expanded play

Sorting grows from two colors to three and four, then to light and dark, then to mixing two paints into a new color basket. A tidy-up mode puts toys back into colored boxes at the end of play. No timer and no score; every finished sort makes the baskets sing.

## Design checks before prototyping

- Learning target: **Color names, sorting by color**. Ask a domain reviewer to validate factual content in the mechanic.
- Keep goals cooperative or self-directed, using resources and needs. Avoid purchasable power. No pressure timers in the accessible mode.
- Use original layouts and character designs; the [legal checklist](../../legal.md) applies even when a mechanic is familiar.
- Choose the minimum [fidelity level](../../simulation-engine.md#determinism-and-fidelity-levels) the lesson needs; concept art does not establish scientific accuracy.
- Review silhouettes, text alternatives, reduced motion and non-color cues against the [adorability gate](../../lore/adorability-gate.md).
- Follow the [toddler design rules](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#7-toddlers-and-screens-r68-r69): tap before drag, no reading, no failure, co-play under age 2.
