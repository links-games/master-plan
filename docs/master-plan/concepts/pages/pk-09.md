# Big Piece Pictures

PK-09 · [Preschool (3–5)](../pk.md) · Cx 2 · Ages 3–5 · Added for the Raya feedback

**The idea:** Six big puzzle pieces lie on a flat beach stone beside a faint outline of a grazer fawn. The child drags a piece near its place, it snaps in with a click, and the finished picture starts to move.

| Decision             | Proposal                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiar mechanic    | Chunky jigsaw puzzles, 6 to 12 pieces                                                                                                                                                       |
| Learning objective   | Part and whole, shapes and spatial thinking                                                                                                                                                 |
| First prototype      | One authored challenge built around the interaction above, with a clear goal, immediate feedback, restart and unlimited retries.                                                            |
| Technology           | React + SVG/DOM; deterministic state, undo and seeded levels. Start with one local screen and touch plus keyboard input.                                                                    |
| Scope boundary       | One board and one rule set; defer accounts, multiplayer, procedural content and platform SDKs.                                                                                              |
| Reuse                | Shared input, saves, accessibility, seeded state and the preschool (3–5) collection shell.                                                                                                  |
| Prototype acceptance | A player can identify the next action from the screen, complete one challenge, explain the practiced idea and restart without help. Check touch and keyboard play at narrow and wide sizes. |
| Main uncertainty     | Test whether the lesson follows from play and whether the first interaction is understandable without reading.                                                                              |

## Story

Akoren found pieces scattered on the big stone. A leg here, an ear there, a tail far away. "What a puzzle," he laughed slowly. He turned one piece, then another. A tail fit next to a back. An ear fit next to a head. Slowly, slowly, a little fawn appeared. When the last piece clicked in, the fawn blinked and stood up on wobbly legs. "Big things are made of small pieces," said Akoren. "And every small piece has its own place."

> **Wisdom:** Every small piece has its own place.

### Gameplay

Six big puzzle pieces lie on a flat beach stone beside a faint outline of a grazer fawn. The child drags a piece near its place, it snaps in with a click, and the finished picture starts to move.

### World

A flat white boulder on the pebble beach serves as a puzzle table. Behind it the river flows slowly, and grazers drink on the far shore. Trees hold white lattice nests, and a white Chord tower rises over the forest. Soft afternoon light, long gentle shadows, and a calm, focused mood. Slightly raised camera over the stone.

### Characters and materials

Pieces are thick and rounded with big tabs, like wooden toys, showing bright pictures of Raya's animals and places. A faint gray ghost outline shows where each piece belongs. Akoren, a tiny glowing acorn in warm orange and icy blue, watches from the stone. A white Orbi lifts pieces with a shimmer. Warm wood, sand and soft green.

### Expanded play

Puzzles grow from 6 to 9 to 12 pieces, and the ghost outline fades as the child gets better. Each finished picture comes alive: a fawn drinks, a heron flies, a tower glows at night. Later finished puzzles hang in a gallery nest in the trees, and a silly mode swaps pieces between two pictures.

## Design checks before prototyping

- Learning target: **Part and whole, shapes and spatial thinking**. Ask a domain reviewer to validate factual content in the mechanic.
- Keep goals cooperative or self-directed, using resources and needs. Avoid purchasable power. No pressure timers in the accessible mode.
- Use original layouts and character designs; the [legal checklist](../../legal.md) applies even when a mechanic is familiar.
- Choose the minimum [fidelity level](../../simulation-engine.md#determinism-and-fidelity-levels) the lesson needs; concept art does not establish scientific accuracy.
- Review silhouettes, text alternatives, reduced motion and non-color cues against the [adorability gate](../../lore/adorability-gate.md).
- Follow the [toddler design rules](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#7-toddlers-and-screens-r68-r69) and keep every instruction voiced; reading is never required.
