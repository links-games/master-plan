**The idea:** A white picnic cloth lies on the pebbles with three hungry friends around it. The child drags a copied apple, a berry bowl and a leaf cup to each friend, who munches happily and hums a thank you.

| Decision             | Proposal                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiar mechanic    | Pretend picnic play                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Learning objective   | Sharing food, one for each friend, plant foods                                                                                                                                              |
| First prototype      | One authored challenge built around the interaction above, with a clear goal, immediate feedback, restart and unlimited retries.                                                            |
| Technology           | React + SVG/DOM; deterministic state, undo and seeded levels. Start with one local screen and touch plus keyboard input.                                                                    |
| Scope boundary       | One board and one rule set; defer accounts, multiplayer, procedural content and platform SDKs.                                                                                              |
| Reuse                | Shared input, saves, accessibility, seeded state and the preschool (3–5) collection shell.                                                                                                  |

| Main uncertainty     | Test whether the lesson follows from play and whether the first interaction is understandable without reading.                                                                              |

## Story

Linky wanted a picnic. He looked at the old apple tree. "May I take one?" he asked. "Copy, don't take," hummed his Orbi. The Orbi scanned an apple and left it on its branch for the birds. In the morning, a basket of copied apples was waiting. Linky spread a white cloth on the pebbles and called his friends. One apple for Soru, one for Tari, one for Verbi, and one for Linky. Everyone munched. A bird pecked the real apple on the tree. "Everybody gets a picnic," said Linky.

> **Wisdom:** Copy, don't take, and everyone gets a share.

### Gameplay

A white picnic cloth lies on the pebbles with three hungry friends around it. The child drags a copied apple, a berry bowl and a leaf cup to each friend, who munches happily and hums a thank you.

### World

A wide, sunny pebble beach with a soft white picnic cloth spread near the water. The river sparkles, and an old apple tree on the bank keeps its fruit for the birds. White lattice nests hang in nearby trees, and a white Chord tower rises in the distance. Bright midday light, cheerful and relaxed.

### Characters and materials

Linky, Soru, Tari and Verbi sit around the cloth. A garden basket holds copied fruit: apples, berries, pears and flatbread, made overnight by the garden assembler and never picked. A white Orbi floats over the basket, eyes coral red and sky blue. Leaf cups and wooden plates are big and round. Sunny yellow, apple red, leaf green and white.

### Expanded play

The first picnic has three friends and simple food. Later more friends arrive, up to ten, and the child sets one plate for each. New picnic spots appear: under a tower, on a sandbar, in a tree nest. A little cooking corner lets the child stir a fruit salad and pour juice, all pretend and all shared.

## Design checks before prototyping

- Learning target: **Sharing food, one for each friend, plant foods**. Ask a domain reviewer to validate factual content in the mechanic.
- Keep goals cooperative or self-directed, using resources and needs. Avoid purchasable power. No pressure timers in the accessible mode.
- Use original layouts and character designs; the [legal checklist](../../legal.md) applies even when a mechanic is familiar.
- Choose the minimum [fidelity level](../../simulation-engine.md#determinism-and-fidelity-levels) the lesson needs; concept art does not establish scientific accuracy.
- Review silhouettes, text alternatives, reduced motion and non-color cues against the [adorability gate](../../lore/adorability-gate.md).
- Follow the [toddler design rules](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#7-toddlers-and-screens-r68-r69) and keep every instruction voiced; reading is never required.
