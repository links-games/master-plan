**The idea:** Top-down view of a giant capital letter pressed as a wide groove into wet river sand. A glowing pebble marks the start, and a sparkling yellow trail follows the child's finger while the Orbi hums the letter's sound.

## Story

Linky wanted to make a letter in the sand. He pressed too fast, and the line went wobbly. The river came up softly and smoothed the sand flat again. "Try again, slowly," hummed his Orbi. Linky started at the glowing pebble. He went down, around, and down again. The line was slow and round and good. The letter shone, and the Orbi sang its sound. This time the river did not wash it away. It waited beside the letter, as if it wanted to listen too.

> **Wisdom:** Slow lines make strong letters.

### Gameplay

Top-down view of a giant capital letter pressed as a wide groove into wet river sand. A glowing pebble marks the start, and a sparkling yellow trail follows the child's finger while the Orbi hums the letter's sound.

### World

A wide river beach of smooth round pebbles and dark wet sand in soft morning light. Clear shallow water ripples over stones. Blue-green trees lean over the bank, holding small white lattice nests in their crowns. Far away a slim white Chord tower rises, twisted like a plant stem, its rings catching the sun. Calm, cool and quiet.

### Characters and materials

Linky, a round chibi friend with a coral-red left eye and a sky-blue right eye, sits in the sand with his red and blue hands ready. A white Orbi sphere floats above with a soft glowing equator. The letter grooves are damp and shiny brown, the trail glows sunshine yellow, and soft gray outlines keep every shape big and clear.

### Expanded play

Each finished letter turns into a little picture made of sand and pebbles: a curling S becomes a stream, an O becomes a pond with a fish. Later the river gently smooths the sand so new letters can appear, then lowercase partners, and finally the first letter of the child's own name, framed by pebbles.

## Design checks before prototyping

- Learning target: **Letter shapes and letter sounds, finger control**. Ask a domain reviewer to validate factual content in the mechanic.
- Keep goals cooperative or self-directed, using resources and needs. Avoid purchasable power. No pressure timers in the accessible mode.
- Use original layouts and character designs; the [legal checklist](../../legal.md) applies even when a mechanic is familiar.
- Choose the minimum [fidelity level](../../simulation-engine.md#determinism-and-fidelity-levels) the lesson needs; concept art does not establish scientific accuracy.
- Review silhouettes, text alternatives, reduced motion and non-color cues against the [adorability gate](../../lore/adorability-gate.md).
- Follow the [toddler design rules](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#7-toddlers-and-screens-r68-r69) and keep every instruction voiced; reading is never required.
