# Clover Peekaboo

TD-02 · [Toddlers (1–3)](../td.md) · Cx 1 · Ages 1–3 · Added for the Raya feedback

**The idea:** A giant four-leaf clover fills the screen and a fawn's ears poke out from behind it. One tap anywhere folds the leaves open to reveal the fawn's happy face as a warm voice says peekaboo.

| Decision             | Proposal                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiar mechanic    | Peekaboo hide and reveal                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Learning objective   | Object permanence, waiting, joy of surprise                                                                                                                                                 |
| First prototype      | One authored challenge built around the interaction above, with a clear goal, immediate feedback, restart and unlimited retries.                                                            |
| Technology           | React + SVG/DOM; deterministic state, undo and seeded levels. Start with one local screen and touch plus keyboard input.                                                                    |
| Scope boundary       | One board and one rule set; defer accounts, multiplayer, procedural content and platform SDKs.                                                                                              |
| Reuse                | Shared input, saves, accessibility, seeded state and the toddlers (1–3) collection shell.                                                                                                   |
| Prototype acceptance | A player can identify the next action from the screen, complete one challenge, explain the practiced idea and restart without help. Check touch and keyboard play at narrow and wide sizes. |
| Main uncertainty     | Test whether the lesson follows from play and whether the first interaction is understandable without reading.                                                                              |

## Story

Where is the fawn? The clover is very big today. Linky looks left. Linky looks right. Something wiggles. Two soft ears peek over a leaf. The leaves open and... peekaboo! There is the fawn, with shiny eyes and a big smile. The fawn hides again. Linky waits. Linky knows the fawn is still there, even when the leaves are closed. Friends who hide are friends who come back. Peekaboo! Peekaboo! And at the end, the fawn curls up behind the clover and goes to sleep.

> **Wisdom:** Hidden friends always come back.

### Gameplay

A giant four-leaf clover fills the screen and a fawn's ears poke out from behind it. One tap anywhere folds the leaves open to reveal the fawn's happy face as a warm voice says peekaboo.

### World

A sunny clover patch seen close up, with leaves as big as umbrellas, dewdrops and tiny daisies. Behind them lies a soft, blurred meadow with a few grazing fawns and the short white Chord tower far away under a pale blue sky. The camera sits at toddler eye level, so everything feels large, warm and safe.

### Characters and materials

Friends hide one at a time: a spotted fawn, Linky with red and blue eyes, a round white Orbi that blinks its coral and sky-blue eyes, sleepy Nully as a soft see-through bubble, and Itsy hugging itself. Covers are clover leaves, a white lattice blanket and a fern. Big shapes, flat colors and soft gray outlines.

### Expanded play

New hiding places appear over time: a fern curtain, a puff of morning mist, a hollow log, and a white lattice blanket that an Orbi lifts. The reveal waits a little longer as the child grows, building happy suspense. A parent can add their own voice for each name, and the last friend to appear always yawns goodnight.

## Design checks before prototyping

- Learning target: **Object permanence, waiting, joy of surprise**. Ask a domain reviewer to validate factual content in the mechanic.
- Keep goals cooperative or self-directed, using resources and needs. Avoid purchasable power. No pressure timers in the accessible mode.
- Use original layouts and character designs; the [legal checklist](../../legal.md) applies even when a mechanic is familiar.
- Choose the minimum [fidelity level](../../simulation-engine.md#determinism-and-fidelity-levels) the lesson needs; concept art does not establish scientific accuracy.
- Review silhouettes, text alternatives, reduced motion and non-color cues against the [adorability gate](../../lore/adorability-gate.md).
- Follow the [toddler design rules](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#7-toddlers-and-screens-r68-r69): tap before drag, no reading, no failure, co-play under age 2.

and [expansion research](../../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-visual-catalog.md).
