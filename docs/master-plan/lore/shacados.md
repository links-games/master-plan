# Shacados — the rusted world

Shacados is the second civilization of [First Contact](first-contact.md):
the planet that Raya meets. The maintainer's
[Raya comment](https://github.com/links-games/master-plan/pull/2#issuecomment-6338585632)
(requirements R73–R75 in the
[case study](../../case-studies/issue-1/requirements.md#raya-shacados-toddlers-and-the-website-r55r76))
makes it much darker than our first draft:

> Shacados should be much darker, it should have no advanced tech, most
> people should be poor and/or exploited, food shortage. Rusty and dusty
> surface, junk everywhere. Energy from oil, coal, gas, and other polluting
> sources, some water sources and forests left but mostly dying and
> polluted, some regions are frozen and barely livable. […] Shacados can
> still travel in space, but in the least advanced way possible, and most
> unsafe. — the maintainer

The earlier description stays true: a violent anti-utopia, controlled by
fear, with "primitive, just enough" space technology
([first comment](https://github.com/links-games/master-plan/pull/2#issuecomment-6189666260)).
The real-world anchors for every element (smog, acid rain, nuclear-pulse
rockets) are in the
[research note](../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#6-shacados--what-least-advanced-and-most-unsafe-space-travel-means).

**Age rule.** Shacados is a setting for **teens and adults**. Toddlers and
children never see Shacados threats
([world rules](first-contact.md#world-rules-of-the-realistic-layer)).
The [Shacados games](../concepts/sh.md) are rated 10+ when they show only
repair, sharing and hope, and 13+ otherwise.

**Tone rule.** Shacados is dark, but the stories are not hopeless and the
people are never villains. The enemy is the system and the scarcity. Every
scene contains at least one person who shares, mends, teaches or refuses to
harm. That is the seed that First Contact grows.

## Shacados at a glance

| Aspect     | Canon                                                       | Detail we propose                                                                                           |

| Technology | No advanced tech                                            | Roughly Earth's 1890s–1960s: steam, diesel, vacuum tubes, radio, a few telephones, punched-card calculators |
| People     | Most are poor and/or exploited                              | A few **Holder** families own the mines, wells, fields and rockets; everyone else works for ration cards    |

| Surface    | Rusty, dusty, junk everywhere                               | Red-brown dust storms, scrap dunes, dead machines left where they broke                                     |
| Energy     | Oil, coal, gas and other polluting sources                  | Coal for heat and power, oil for engines, gas flares day and night, peat and wood where any is left         |
| Nature     | Some water and forests left, mostly dying and polluted      | Gray rivers, acid rain, a few hidden green valleys guarded by their communities                             |
| Climate    | Some regions frozen and barely livable                      | A long cold age: the north and south are ice; people crowd into the warm belt and the smoke                 |
| Space      | Can still travel, in the least advanced and most unsafe way | Chemical rockets with high failure rates; crude nuclear-pulse boosters launched from the ground             |

## The planet

- **Star and orbit** _(proposal)_: Shacados circles an orange star, a little
  cooler and dimmer than the Sun, in a slightly wider orbit than would be
  comfortable. The planet is cold by nature. Its civilization survived by
  burning everything.
- **The frozen regions:** two thirds of the land is tundra or ice sheet.
  Some old towns are buried under snow, and their chimneys still stand out
  of it. A few people live there anyway, in the ice towns around coal seams.
- **The warm belt:** a strip of land near the equator, crowded with
  smokestacks, rail yards and shanty towns. This is where most people live.
- **The sky** is brown-gray by day and red at sunset because of soot and
  sulfur. On rare clear nights after a storm, people climb roofs to see the
  stars. One of those faint stars is Raya's sun.
- **Water:** the big rivers are gray with mine waste and dye. Clean water
  comes from deep wells, which are owned, and from rain, which is acid.
  Every family has a homemade filter of sand, charcoal and cloth.
- **Forests:** most forests were cut for mine timber or killed by acid rain.
  The **Last Woods** _(proposal)_ survive in a few valleys. They are
  protected in secret by the people who live there, and they are the most
  beautiful places in Shacados art.
- **Junk:** nothing is collected, so broken machines stay where they died.
  Children play in scrap fields. Scavengers ("rakers") live by sorting
  metal, glass and wire.

## Society

### The Holders and the Hands

- **Holders** _(proposal)_ are a few dozen families. Each owns a monopoly
  ("a Hold"): coal, oil, grain, water, rails, radio or rockets. Holders
  compete with each other, and their workers pay the price.
- **Hands** are everyone else. They are paid in ration cards that are
  valid only in the company store. Debts pass from parents to children.
  Hands who complain lose their ration card. That is the fear that
  controls them.
- **Wardens** are Hands paid to keep the others in line. Most of them are
  scared too.
- **The violence** of the anti-utopia is real and mostly structural: hunger,
  cold, debt, dangerous work, lack of medicine. In our games it is
  **implied, never shown**. No player ever harms anyone, and no harm is
  ever a reward.

### What keeps people human

These are the hopeful threads that every Shacados story carries:

1. **Sharing heat.** In the cold months, families sleep in one room around
   one stove. Neighbors share coal. A shared fire is the oldest promise in
   Shacados.
2. **Seed keepers.** Secret networks keep old seeds of trees and crops in
   jars, waiting for clean soil. Their saying: "Plant for someone you will
   never meet."
3. **Menders.** Repairing things is a respected art. A good mender can fix
   a radio from three dead ones.
4. **Night schools.** Teachers who lost their jobs teach children to read by
   lamplight, in exchange for soup.
5. **The radio listeners.** Hobbyists build receivers from scrap and listen
   to the sky. They will be the first to hear Raya.

### People

_(proposal)_ Shacados people are humanlike, like Rayans. That is the shock
of First Contact: the same kind of people, living in two very different
worlds. Shacadosi look older than their years. Their clothes are layered,
patched work cloth in rust, soot and mustard colors, with scarves against
the dust and goggles against the glare of the gas flares. Few have shoes
that fit.

## Technology and energy

| Area          | Shacados                                                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Power         | Coal plants, oil engines, gas flares, wood and peat stoves                                 |
| Transport     | Steam and diesel trains, rusty trucks, carts; most people walk                             |
| Communication | Telegraph and radio owned by the Radio Hold; homemade receivers are illegal but everywhere |
| Computation   | Rooms of clerks with mechanical calculators; one vacuum-tube computer per Hold             |
| Medicine      | Basic surgery, few antibiotics, folk remedies                                              |
| Industry      | Mining, smelting, refining; everything leaks                                               |
| Food          | Tubers, grain on poisoned soil, cave fungus, canned rations                                |

### Spaceflight — the least advanced and most unsafe way

Shacados reaches space the way a desperate civilization would:

- **Chemical rockets** built from riveted steel and kerosene. Many explode
  on the pad. The crews are debt workers who fly to clear their families'
  debts.
- **Crude nuclear-pulse boosters**: a heavy steel pusher plate with bombs
  exploding behind it, one by one, launched **from the ground**. This is the
  real Project Orion idea that Earth abandoned partly because of fallout
  ([research note](../../case-studies/issue-1/data/research-raya-shacados.md#6-shacados--what-least-advanced-and-most-unsafe-space-travel-means)).
  Every launch poisons a region. The Rocket Hold chooses regions where the
  poorest Hands live.
- **No life support worth the name.** Crewed flights are short, and crews
  return sick, if they return at all.
- **Remote control** by radio from a crew vehicle trailing behind a probe,
  because radio lag makes control from home impossible. This explains the
  pilot scene's need for a nearby controller
  ([physics check](first-contact.md#physics-check-of-the-pilot)).
- **The first probe** _(proposal)_: an uncrewed nuclear-pulse probe, pushed
  by hundreds of dirty bombs to about 10,000 km/s (Dyson's optimistic limit
  for such a design), aimed at the nearest star system. It is the object
  in the pilot scene. Nobody on Shacados knows that the target system is
  inhabited. They hope to find resources.

Why does a starving world spend on rockets? Because the Holders want new
mines in the sky. They believe space is the only place left to take from.
The tragedy, and the hope, of First Contact is that the first thing they
find is a world that never takes.

## Visual guide

| Element   | Rule                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Palette   | Rust `#8A4B2A`, soot `#3B3B3B`, mustard `#C9A227`, ash `#B8B2A7`, sick green `#7A8C4A`, and one warm lamp orange `#F2A65A` for hope |
| Light     | Low, smoky, orange-brown by day; gas-flare glare at night; one warm light source in every scene (a stove, a lamp, a candle)         |
| Materials | Rusted steel, cracked concrete, patched canvas, soot-stained brick, oily puddles, frost on metal                                    |
| Nature    | Thin dying trees, gray rivers, and always one small living green thing for the player to protect                                    |
| People    | Layered patched clothes, scarves, goggles; tired faces with dignity; never shown as monsters                                        |
| Never     | Gore, weapons in use, corpses, cruelty as spectacle, or any harm caused by the player                                               |

Contrast with Raya: Raya is white, open, quiet and green; Shacados is
brown, crowded, loud and gray. Put side by side, the two palettes tell
the story without words.
