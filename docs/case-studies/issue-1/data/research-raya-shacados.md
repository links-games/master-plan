# Research: Raya, Shacados and toddler games (R55–R76)

This note backs the lore and planning added for the maintainer's
[Raya comment](https://github.com/links-games/master-plan/pull/2#issuecomment-6338585632)
(raw copy: [pr-2-raya-feedback.json](pr-2-raya-feedback.json)). Each section
lists the real-world anchor, what it proves, and where the lore deliberately
goes beyond it. The lore documents call such a step a **lore exception**:
the realistic layer of
[First Contact](../../../master-plan/lore/first-contact.md#world-rules-of-the-realistic-layer)
allows exceptions only with a written justification, and this note is that
justification.

## 1. Acoustic levitation and acoustic "flying"

- **Anchor.** Marzo, Seah, Drinkwater, Sahoo, Long and Subramanian,
  "Holographic acoustic elements for manipulation of levitated objects",
  _Nature Communications_ 6, 8661 (2015),
  [doi:10.1038/ncomms9661](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9661). Phased arrays
  of 40 kHz ultrasonic emitters trap, rotate and move millimeter-sized beads
  in mid-air with tractor beams shaped as "acoustic holograms".
- **What it proves.** Sound can hold and move objects without contact, from
  one side, under software control. Phased arrays steer the focus in real
  time — the same principle Raya's towers use.
- **Where the lore goes further.** Levitating a person needs a radiation
  force of hundreds of newtons. In air, the sound intensity this requires
  would be deafening and would heat skin. **Lore exception E1 (Chord flight):**
  tower arrays push only on the rigid lattice panels of a flight suit, which
  reflect the field and isolate the body; the suit, not the skin, takes the
  force. Flight is therefore possible only near towers, slow (walking to
  cycling speed), and silent to the wearer. Without a suit, Raya has no
  flight.

## 2. Diamagnetic levitation — how an Orbi lifts an apple

- **Anchor.** M. V. Berry and A. K. Geim, "Of flying frogs and levitrons",
  _European Journal of Physics_ 18 (1997) 307–313. A live frog floated
  unharmed in a field of about 16 tesla, because water — and so most living
  tissue — is weakly diamagnetic. Geim and Berry received the 2000 Ig Nobel
  Prize in Physics for it. The Nijmegen lab also floated strawberries, water
  drops and hazelnuts.
- **What it proves.** An apple (mostly water) can float in a strong field
  without being touched or harmed. Combined with acoustic tweezers for fine
  positioning, this is exactly the maintainer's "electromagnetic and acoustic
  levitation".
- **Where the lore goes further.** The real fields come from megawatt-class
  laboratory magnets. **Lore exception E2 (Orbi field):** a 30 cm Orbi sphere
  shapes an equivalent field locally for a few seconds, using room-temperature
  superconductors and stored energy. It recharges from the Chord towers. The
  field is small: about one meter across, and a few kilograms at most. Lifting
  a person is the towers' job (E1), never an Orbi's.

## 3. Reading and writing the brain — the "telepathy internet"

- **Reading.** Boto et al., "Moving magnetoencephalography towards real-world
  applications with a wearable system", _Nature_ 555, 657–661 (2018). Optically
  pumped magnetometers record brain activity from a wearable helmet while the
  person moves.
- **Writing.** Legon et al., "Transcranial focused ultrasound modulates the
  activity of primary somatosensory cortex in humans", _Nature Neuroscience_
  17, 322–329 (2014). Focused ultrasound changes cortical activity without
  surgery.
- **Muscle control.** Barker, Jalinous and Freeston, "Non-invasive magnetic
  stimulation of human motor cortex", _The Lancet_ 1985; 1(8437):1106–1107,
  [doi:10.1016/s0140-6736(85)92413-4](<https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(85)92413-4>).
  A magnetic pulse over the motor cortex makes a hand muscle twitch.
  Functional electrical stimulation (FES) moves paralyzed limbs in clinical
  practice today.
- **Where the lore goes further.** Today's systems read coarse signals and
  write blunt pulses. **Lore exception E4 (the Chord):** an Orbi reads and
  writes at the level of words, images and intentions, with consent, and
  uses an acoustic plus electromagnetic link. It can also **pause** a motor
  command before the muscle moves (the Guard), but it never writes thoughts.
  "Thoughts are free" is a design limit written into every Orbi, not a
  technical limitation. It is the deepest rule of Rayan society.

## 4. Copying an apple without taking it

- **Anchors.** Non-destructive scanning is real at the cell scale:
  micro-CT, MRI and ultrasound imaging all see inside fruit without cutting
  it. Building objects from a scan is real at the macro scale in 3D printing
  and bioprinting.
- **Where the lore goes further.** **Lore exception E3 (Copy):** a Rayan
  garden assembler builds an atom-accurate copy from a scan. Carbon comes
  from air (CO₂), water from the ground and minerals from compost; energy
  comes from the towers' solar and geothermal grid. A copy of a 150 g apple
  takes **one night**. That is slow on purpose: it keeps copying honest and
  visible, and it shows that energy and matter are never free. The original
  apple stays on the tree, unharmed, for the birds.

## 5. Staying young — Renewal

- **Anchors.** López-Otín et al., "The hallmarks of aging", _Cell_ 153 (2013)
  1194–1217, frames aging as a set of measurable, partly reversible
  processes. Ocampo et al., "In vivo amelioration of age-associated hallmarks
  by partial reprogramming", _Cell_ 167 (2016) 1719–1733, reversed signs of
  aging in mice by briefly expressing Yamanaka factors. The hydrozoan
  _Turritopsis dohrnii_ can revert to a juvenile stage, so biological
  "immortality" exists in nature.
- **Where the lore goes further.** **Lore exception E5 (Renewal):** Orbis
  run continuous, gentle partial reprogramming. They keep adult bodies at an
  apparent age of 18–21 and repair injuries. Renewal is a default that each
  citizen can switch off. Those who switch it off age naturally; they are
  rare and honored elders.

## 6. Shacados — what "least advanced and most unsafe" space travel means

- **Anchor.** Freeman J. Dyson, "Interstellar Transport", _Physics Today_,
  October 1968, pp. 41–45. A nuclear-pulse ship (Project Orion) explodes
  bombs behind a pusher plate. Dyson's crude "heat-sink" design reaches about
  1,000 km/s, and his optimistic "ablation" design reaches about
  10,000 km/s (about c/30). Orion was cancelled in 1965, partly because
  launching from the ground spreads fallout. The 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty
  made it illegal.
- **Chemical rockets.** The fastest human-made objects are close-pass solar
  probes. Parker Solar Probe reached about 191 km/s at perihelion in 2024.
  Chemical rockets can never reach thousands of km/s.
- **Consequence for the pilot scene.** The pilot's object flies at
  40,000 km/s. That is four times Dyson's optimistic limit and impossible
  for a "primitive, just enough" civilization. Our proposal: the Shacados
  probe is a crude nuclear-pulse vehicle at about **10,000 km/s**. It was
  pushed by hundreds of dirty bombs, and its launch poisoned a region of
  its own planet. Detection at **500,000 km** keeps the scene's 50-second
  window. The numbers are checked by
  [`examples/first-contact-physics.mjs`](../../../../examples/first-contact-physics.mjs)
  and its test.
- **Industrial anchors** for Shacados' surface come from Earth's own history:
  coal smog (the London smog of December 1952 caused about 4,000 deaths in
  days, later estimates up to 12,000), oil fires, dying forests from acid
  rain (Central Europe's "Waldsterben" in the 1980s), dead rivers, company
  towns and debt bondage. Shacados is darker than any single case, but
  every element has happened somewhere on Earth.

## 7. Toddlers and screens (R68, R69)

- **WHO (2019),** _Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and
  sleep for children under 5 years of age_
  ([WHO publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550536)):
  no sedentary screen time under age 2; for ages 2–4, no more than one hour
  a day, and less is better. Interactive activities with a caregiver are
  encouraged.
- **AAP (2016),** "Media and Young Minds", _Pediatrics_ 138(5): e20162591. It
  advises avoiding digital media, except video chat, under 18–24 months. For
  ages 18–24 months it advises high-quality content watched together with a
  parent, and for ages 2–5 one hour a day of high-quality programming,
  co-viewed.
- **Hourcade, Mascher, Wu and Pantoja (CHI 2015),** "Look, my baby is using an
  iPad! An analysis of YouTube videos of infants and toddlers using tablets".
  The study analyzed videos of children younger than 3 using tablets. Most
  children aged 12–17 months showed moderate ability, and over 90% of
  two-year-olds did. Children move from a whole hand to a single index
  finger around their first birthday.
- **Cristia and Seidl (2015)**, as summarized in the 2016 _Frontiers in
  Psychology_ review of touch-screen use by young children: among toddlers,
  tap was the most common gesture (71% could do it), then drag (41%) and
  swipe (20%).

**Design consequences** used in the
[toddler collection](../../../master-plan/concepts/td.md) and the
[learnability rubric](../../../master-plan/development-plan.md#toddler-learnability-rubric):

1. Ages 1–2 are **co-play only**: a parent holds the device, the game invites
   pointing, naming and singing, and it ends itself after a few minutes.
2. Tap before drag before swipe; one target at a time for ages 1–2; large
   targets (at least 2 cm on screen); no multi-touch requirements.
3. No fail state, no timer, no text, no reading, no ads, no purchases
   anywhere a child can reach.
4. Every action gives an instant, gentle response (sound, motion, color),
   because cause and effect is the core joy at this age.
5. Sessions end with a calm "goodbye" scene, never with a cliffhanger.
